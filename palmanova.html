Palmanova

"The Renaissance Ideal Star City"

Historical Context

Built by the Venetian Republic in 1593 to commemorate the victory over the Ottoman Empire at Lepanto, Palmanova was designed as a military masterpiece. Constructed as a strict nine-pointed star polygon (nonagon), its symmetrical ramparts and bastions were designed so that every point of defense could be covered by overlapping crossfire.

Key Elements

Nine-Pointed Bastions

Complex radial earthworks and stone fortifications engineered to absorb artillery and eliminate defensive blind spots.

Piazza Grande

A perfectly hexagonal central parade square from which radial streets extend to the city gates.

History

Located in northeastern Italy, Palmanova was engineered in the late 16th century by Giulio Savorgnan to protect Venetian territories from Ottoman and Austrian incursions. Its unique geometry allowed defenders in the bastions to provide overlapping crossfire, and the fortress evolved over centuries to eventually feature three defensive rings.

Wonders of Palmanova

  • The Monumental Gates: Historic entry points like Porta Udine, which still preserves ancient iron drawbridge mechanisms.
  • Galleria Contromina: Intricate underground counter-mine tunnels running beneath the outer fortifications that visitors can explore.
  • Ramparts and Moats: Extensive, preserved green spaces along the outer walls ideal for scenic walks, jogging, and cycling.

Travel Information

  • Best time: Ideal for year-round walking, jogging, and cycling tours.
  • Tips: The city is compact and pedestrian-friendly; it is best explored on foot or by bicycle.
  • Transport: Directly accessible via the A4/A23 highway or regional railway connections from Udine, Trieste, and Venice.
