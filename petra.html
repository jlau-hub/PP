Petra

"The Rose-Red City of Nabataeans"

Historical Context

Petra served as the booming capital of the Nabataean kingdom from roughly 400 BCE to 106 CE. Nestled inside a dramatic desert canyon network in southern Jordan, the city prospered due to Nabataean mastery over rainwater harvesting and their strategic control of Arabian trade routes.

Famous Monuments

Al-Khazneh (The Treasury)

A 40-meter-tall Hellenistic facade carved directly out of the cliff wall, greeting travelers at the mouth of the Siq canyon.

Ad-Deir (The Monastery)

A massive mountain-top monument built in the 1st century CE that later served as a Christian chapel.

History

Established around the 4th century BCE by the Nabataeans—an industrious Arab nomadic tribe—Petra grew into a major trading hub by controlling crucial ancient caravan routes transporting frankincense, myrrh, and spices from southern Arabia to the Mediterranean. Despite its desert surroundings, the Nabataeans engineered an advanced hydraulic system of dams, cisterns, and terracotta water conduits to capture flash floods and sustain a thriving urban population. In 106 CE, the Roman Empire annexed the kingdom, expanding the city with classic civic structures like a colonnaded street and a grand open-air theater.

Wonders of Petra

  • The Siq: A dramatic, natural gateway over 1 kilometer long flanked by towering cliffs.
  • Al-Khazneh (The Treasury): The iconic, intricately carved Hellenistic facade greeting visitors as they emerge from the Siq.
  • The Monastery (Ad-Deir): A colossal monument perched high in the hills, accessed by climbing over 800 ancient rock-cut stairs.
  • Street of Facades: A cliffside avenue lined with numerous monumental Nabataean tombs featuring elaborate decorative carvings.

Travel Information

  • Best time: Spring (March–May) and autumn offer cooler daytime temperatures for hiking.
  • Tips: Wear sturdy walking shoes, carry plenty of water, and consider a 2-day pass to thoroughly experience the vast site.
  • Transport: Easily accessible via the Desert Highway or the scenic King's Highway from Amman (about a 3-hour drive) or Aqaba.
