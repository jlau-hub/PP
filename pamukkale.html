Pamukkale

"The Cotton Castle and Ancient Spa"

Natural & Historical Significance

Pamukkale ("Cotton Castle" in Turkish) is a natural wonder of gleaming white travertine terraces formed by mineral-rich thermal springs overflowing down cliff faces. Situated directly above the terraces lies Hierapolis, an ancient Greco-Roman spa city founded by the Attalid kings of Pergamon in the 2nd century BCE.

Highlights

Travertine Terraces

Cascading mineral pools filled with warm, calcite-saturated thermal waters.

Hierapolis Ruins

Well-preserved Greco-Roman ruins including a massive amphitheater and the Antique Pool with submerged Roman columns.

History

Pamukkale's iconic landscape is shaped by 17 hot springs emerging from a cliff nearly 200 meters high, where water rich in calcium bicarbonate cools to form dazzling white shelves. Perched atop this formation is Hierapolis, founded around 190 BCE by Eumenes II, which flourished under Roman rule as a premier therapeutic spa destination attracting dignitaries and the infirm to its sacred mineral waters.

Wonders of Pamukkale

  • The Antique Pool (Cleopatra's Pool): A submerged thermal pool where visitors swim amidst fallen ancient Roman marble columns.
  • The Great Theater: A remarkably preserved 2nd-century Roman theater with seating for up to 20,000 spectators.
  • The Necropolis: One of Turkey's largest ancient cemeteries, stretching over 2 kilometers with sarcophagi and house-tombs.
  • Pluto's Gate (Plutonium): Historically revered as the mythological 'Gate to Hell' due to subterranean carbon dioxide emissions.

Travel Information

  • Best time: Spring water temperatures range from 35°C to 100°C year-round.
  • Tips: Strict regulations govern footwear and public access on active terraces to protect the white mineral formations from pollution.
  • Transport: Fly into Denizli Çardak Airport (DNZ), approximately 65 km away.
